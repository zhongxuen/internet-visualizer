# Caching, TTLs, and saying no

The walk you learned in lesson 1 — root, then TLD, then authoritative — almost never
happens. If it did, the root servers would field every DNS query on Earth and the system
would have collapsed in about 1985.

What happens instead is that somebody already asked. Watch the same question twice:

## A hit skips the whole hierarchy, not part of it

The second lookup contacted nobody. That is the shape of the saving: a

<Term>cache</Term> hit does not shorten the walk, it removes it entirely, and the answer
comes back before the first attempt had finished talking to the root.

Two details decide how well this works:

- The <Term>TTL</Term> counts **down** as an answer is passed along, and never restarts.
  A record with 300 seconds left is served with 300 seconds left, not 300 fresh ones.
- Delegations and glue are cached separately from answers, and usually for far longer.
  So even a genuine miss on `www.example.com` normally skips the root and the TLD, and
  goes straight to the authoritative server.

## Caching "no"

Negative answers get cached too, and this surprises people. When a name genuinely does
not exist, the <Term>NXDOMAIN</Term> comes back with an SOA record from the closest
enclosing zone, and a field in that SOA says how long the "no" may be remembered.

Without it, every typo, every stale link and every misconfigured client in the world
would walk the tree from the root, forever, on every attempt.
